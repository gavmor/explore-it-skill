# Elisabeth Hendrickson

**Elisabeth Hendrickson** is a prominent software engineering and testing leader, agile practitioner, and the author of *[[references/explore-it|Explore It! Reduce Risk and Increase Confidence with Exploratory Testing]]*. She is widely recognized for bridging the gap between developers and testers and bringing disciplined [[concepts/exploratory-testing|exploratory testing]] into agile software development.

## Background and Career

- **Early Testing Career & LAWST:** Participated in the Los Altos Workshop on Software Testing (LAWST) founded by Cem Kaner, collaborating with testing thinkers such as Jerry Weinberg, James Bach, Jon Bach, Dale Emery, and James Lyndsay.
- **Quality Tree Software:** Founded Quality Tree Software, publishing early heuristics cheat sheets and testing tutorials through *Test Obsessed*.
- **Pivotal Labs & Extreme Programming:** Worked at Pivotal Labs in San Francisco, helping define how exploratory testing integrates alongside Extreme Programming (XP) practices—specifically Test-Driven Development (TDD), pair programming, continuous integration, and rapid automated build pipelines (see [[concepts/exploratory-testing-in-agile|exploratory testing in agile]]).
- **Executive Leadership:** Later served in engineering executive roles (including VP of R&D at Pivotal), advancing cloud-native platforms and developer productivity.

## Core Contributions to Testing

1. **Tested = Checked + Explored:** Articulated the essential dual nature of testing, removing the false dichotomy between automated checks and manual exploration.
2. **Charter-Driven Exploration:** Popularized and refined the structured 3-part [[concepts/test-charter|test charter]] template (`Explore [target] with [resources] to discover [information]`).
3. **The Heuristics Cheat Sheet:** Systematized widely accessible cognitive heuristics for test design across input data, sequence, state, and ecosystem dimensions (see [[concepts/test-heuristics|test heuristics]]).
4. **Testability & Observability:** Advocated for "making the invisible visible" through application logs, private interfaces, diagnostic consoles, and runtime testability hooks (see [[concepts/software-observability-testing|software observability in testing]]).
